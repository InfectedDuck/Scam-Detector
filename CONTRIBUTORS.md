## Being listed

Anyone who annotates a batch, corrects a playbook, fixes the Kazakh, or reports a real vulnerability belongs on this table. Ask, or add yourself in your pull request — you do not need permission.

**A pseudonym is fine, and so is no name at all.** Working with scam data is not something everyone wants attached to their real name in a public repository, and that is a reasonable position. Say how you want to appear:

- your full name;
- a first name and an initial;
- a handle or pseudonym;
- `Anonymous annotator`, with the role and language columns filled in.

The last option still counts. Inter-annotator agreement is reported per annotator in the dataset's datasheet, and it does not need a real name to be meaningful.

To be removed, or to change how you appear, open an issue or a pull request. No reason needed.

## Annotators and the datasheet

Annotator names listed here also appear in the KazScam datasheet, which records who labelled which split and the agreement between them. If you would rather appear in one and not the other, say so and it will be honoured.

## Also with thanks

Reports of real scam messages from people who ticked the consent box. Those submissions are anonymous by construction — the tool never learns who sent them — so they cannot be credited individually here. They are, nonetheless, what the dataset is made of.
